# Spanish Grammar

## Pronunciation & spelling

- Vowels are pure: `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u` — one short sound each. Read what's written.
- `h` is always silent: `hospital`, `ahora`, `horrible`, `hacer`.
- `v` is pronounced as a soft `b`: `verano`, `vive`, `voy`.
- `c` before `e`/`i`: `/θ/` in most of Spain, `/s/` in Latin America (`gracias`, `cinco`).
- `z`: same as `c` + `e`/`i` (`/θ/` Spain, `/s/` Latin America) — `empezar`, `razón`, `hizo`.
- `g` before `e`/`i` and `j` sound the same: `/x/` (`general`, `gente`, `rojo`, `dijo`).
- Single `r` between vowels is a tap; `rr` / initial `r` is a trill (`pero` ≠ `perro`, `caro` ≠ `carro`).
- Spanish doesn't double consonants except `rr`, `ll`, `nn` (passion → `pasión`; possible → `posible`).
- `ñ` = `/ny/` (`mañana`, `año`).
- `q-u` is always `/k/` (never `/kw/`): `que`, `quiero`, `quedar`, `queso`.
- English `s` + consonant at the start of loans gets a leading `e-` (student → `estudiante`, special → `especial`, scheme → `esquema`).

### Default stress and written accents

| Word ends in                    | Default stress                | Example                       | Accent needed when                           |
|---------------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| vowel, `n`, or `s`              | Penultimate syllable          | `casa`, `hablan`, `vives`     | stress is elsewhere → write it (`tradición`) |
| any other consonant (incl. `r`) | Last syllable                 | `español`, `hablar`, `normal` | stress is elsewhere → write it (`lápiz`)     |
| —                               | Verb endings `-é`, `-í`, `-ó` | `hablé`, `comí`, `vivió`      | always written                               |

### Monosyllable accent pairs

| Without accent                      | With accent                         |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| `si` — if                           | `sí` — yes                          |
| `mi` — my                           | `mí` — me (after preposition)       |
| `tu` — your                         | `tú` — you                          |
| `se` — reflexive / IO clitic        | `sé` — I know                       |
| `donde`/`como`/`cuando` — relatives | `dónde`/`cómo`/`cuándo` — questions |

## Gender, articles, and `lo`

- Gender belongs to the WORD, not to the physical object.
- Default cues: `-o` ending → masculine; `-a` ending → feminine.
- `-ión`, `-ad`, `-tad`, `-tud` nouns are usually feminine (`la opinión`, `la diversidad`).
- Greek `-ma` loans are masculine: `el problema`, `el sistema`, `el programa`, `el clima`, `el panorama`, `el paradigma`.
- True feminine `-o` nouns: `la mano`, `la foto` (`fotografía`), `la moto` (`motocicleta`).
- Some feminine `-a` nouns take `el` for sound when stressed first vowel is `a` (`el agua`, `el ala`, `el aula`); the adjective stays feminine: `el agua fría`.
- Neuter `lo` + adjective = 'the X thing': `lo importante`, `lo increíble`, `lo interesante`.
- `lo de` + noun = the matter of (`lo de la fiesta`).
- `a` + `el` = `al`.
- `de` + `el` = `del`.

### Articles

|            | Masc. sg | Fem. sg | Masc. pl | Fem. pl | Neuter             |
|------------|----------|---------|----------|---------|--------------------|
| Definite   | `el`     | `la`    | `los`    | `las`   | `lo` (+ adjective) |
| Indefinite | `un`     | `una`   | `unos`   | `unas`  | —                  |

## Nouns and plurals

- Vowel-final → add `s` (`casa` → `casas`).
- Consonant-final → add `es` (`profesor` → `profesores`, `usted` → `ustedes`).
- `z` → `c` before `e`: `vez` → `veces`, `luz` → `luces`.
- Adjectives, articles, possessives all match the noun in gender and number.

References: 12, 24, 27, 28, 65

## Subject pronouns and formality

- Spanish drops subject pronouns; use them only for emphasis or contrast.
- Spain uses `tú` / `vosotros` for informal you (sg/pl); Latin America uses `tú` / `ustedes`.
- `usted` / `ustedes` are formal — they conjugate with 3rd-person endings.
- `voseo` (Río de la Plata): `vos` replaces `tú` with end-stressed forms (`podés`, `querés`).

### Subject pronouns

| Person     | Singular    | Plural                             |
|------------|-------------|------------------------------------|
| 1          | `yo`        | `nosotros`/`nosotras`              |
| 2 informal | `tú`        | `vosotros`/`vosotras` (Spain only) |
| 2 formal   | `usted`     | `ustedes`                          |
| 3          | `él`/`ella` | `ellos`/`ellas`                    |

References: 5, 8, 22, 24, 90

## Adjectives, comparatives, intensifiers

- Adjectives agree in gender AND number (`caro`/`cara`/`caros`/`caras`).
- Most descriptive adjectives go AFTER the noun (`cámara digital`, `casa grande`).
- Past participles can be used as adjectives with `estar`: `la silla está mojada`.
- English `-ing` adjective → `-ante` or `-iente` (`interesante`, `frustrante`, `viviente`).
- `tan` + adjective = `so ...` ; `tan` + adjective + `que` = `so ... that`.
- `más` + adjective + `que` = `more than` (`Comí más que él`).
- `demasiado` agrees with a following noun (`demasiadas casas`) but stays masc.sg before another adjective (`demasiado agresivo`).
- Drop final `-o` before a masc.sg noun: `uno` → `un`, `alguno` → `algún`, `ninguno` → `ningún` (also `bueno` → `buen`, `malo` → `mal`, `primero` → `primer`).

References: 27, 28, 33, 52, 54, 59, 65, 68, 89

## Possessives and demonstratives

- Possessives agree with the THING owned, not the owner: `mi`/`mis`, `tu`/`tus`, `su`/`sus`, `nuestro`(`a`/`os`/`as`), `vuestro`(`a`/`os`/`as`).
- `su` can mean `his`/`her`/`its`/`your`(formal)/`their` — context decides. Disambiguate with `de` + person.
- Possession via `de`: `la casa de José`, `la fiesta de María`.
- `propio`: `mi propia casa`, `mis propios ojos`.
- Demonstratives agree in gender/number; neuter (`esto`/`eso`/`aquello`) refers to an idea or unknown thing.

### Demonstratives

| Distance      | Masc.sg | Fem.sg    | Masc.pl    | Fem.pl     | Neuter    |
|---------------|---------|-----------|------------|------------|-----------|
| this (here)   | `este`  | `esta`    | `estos`    | `estas`    | `esto`    |
| that (there)  | `ese`   | `esa`     | `esos`     | `esas`     | `eso`     |
| that (yonder) | `aquel` | `aquella` | `aquellos` | `aquellas` | `aquello` |

References: 44, 47, 65, 66, 68

## `ser` vs `estar`

- `ser` for what something IS (characteristic: traits, identity, origin, profession, time, material, possession).
- `estar` for state, location, mood, ongoing action (`estado`).
- `estar` present is end-stressed in most forms (`estoy`, `estás`, `está`, `estamos`, `están`) — only 4 `yo` `-oy` verbs total: `voy`, `doy`, `soy`, `estoy`.
- Origin uses `ser` (`¿De dónde eres?`); current location uses `estar` (`¿Dónde estás?`).
- `estar` + past participle for resulting state: `la puerta está cerrada`.
- Some adjectives shift meaning: `listo` (smart vs ready), `aburrido` (boring vs bored), `bueno` (good vs tasty/attractive), `libre` (free).
- When in doubt, rephrase as `soy una persona` + adjective to force `ser`.

### `ser` and `estar` — present indicative

|                       | ser     | estar     |
|-----------------------|---------|-----------|
| `yo`                  | `soy`   | `estoy`   |
| `tú`                  | `eres`  | `estás`   |
| `él`/`ella`/`usted`   | `es`    | `está`    |
| `nosotros`/`nosotras` | `somos` | `estamos` |
| `ellos`/`ustedes`     | `son`   | `están`   |

References: 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 39, 54, 55

## `haber`: `hay`, perfects, `había`, `habría`

- `hay` = `there is/are` (one form for both); past `había` = `there was/were`.
- `tener que haber` / `debería haber` + noun = `there should/must be`.
- `haber` is the auxiliary of all compound (perfect) tenses: `haber` + past participle.
- Conjugated forms across tenses: `he`, `has`, `ha`, `hemos`, `han` (present); `había-...` (imperfect); `habré-...` (future); `habría-...` (conditional); `haya-...` (subjunctive); `hubiera-...` (past subjunctive).
- Object pronouns ALWAYS go before `haber`: `lo he visto` (not `he lo visto`).

### `haber` across tenses

|                     | Present (perfect) | Imperfect (pluperfect) | Future (fut. perf.) | Conditional (cond. perf.) | Subjunctive | Past subjunctive          |
|---------------------|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------|---------------------------|-------------|---------------------------|
| `yo`                | `he`              | `había`                | `habré`             | `habría`                  | `haya`      | `hubiera`/`hubiese`       |
| `tú`                | `has`             | `habías`               | `habrás`            | `habrías`                 | `hayas`     | `hubieras`/`hubieses`     |
| `él`/`ella`/`usted` | `ha` (`hay`)      | `había`                | `habrá`             | `habría`                  | `haya`      | `hubiera`/`hubiese`       |
| `nosotros`          | `hemos`           | `habíamos`             | `habremos`          | `habríamos`               | `hayamos`   | `hubiéramos`/`hubiésemos` |
| `ellos`/`ustedes`   | `han`             | `habían`               | `habrán`            | `habrían`                 | `hayan`     | `hubieran`/`hubiesen`     |

References: 21, 22, 23, 43, 46, 53, 76, 77, 83

## Verb conjugation overview

- Three groups of infinitives: `-ar`, `-er`, `-ir`.
- Group key vowel: `-ar` uses `a` in present endings; `-er`/`-ir` use `e` (with one structural split in `nosotros`).
- Person markers: `-o` (`yo`), `-s` (`tú`), nothing (`él`/`ella`/`usted`), `-mos` (`nosotros`), `-is` (`vosotros`), `-n` (`ellos`/`ustedes`).
- Always go back to the infinitive before applying any rule.

### Present indicative — regular endings

|                       | `-ar` (`hablar`) | `-er` (`comer`) | `-ir` (`vivir`) |
|-----------------------|------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| `yo`                  | `hablo`          | `como`          | `vivo`          |
| `tú`                  | `hablas`         | `comes`         | `vives`         |
| `él`/`ella`/`usted`   | `habla`          | `come`          | `vive`          |
| `nosotros`/`nosotras` | `hablamos`       | `comemos`       | `vivimos`       |
| `vosotros`/`nosotras` | `habláis`        | `coméis`        | `vivís`         |
| `ellos`/`ustedes`     | `hablan`         | `comen`         | `viven`         |

References: 10, 11, 13, 18, 57, 60

## Stem-changing verbs (present)

- Stress-driven: a stressed vowel in the root may split. The `nosotros`/`vosotros` forms keep the original vowel (stress moves to the ending).
- `e` → `ie`: `querer` → `quiero`, `pensar` → `pienso`, `perder` → `pierdo`, `entender` → `entiendo`, `empezar` → `empiezo`, `preferir` → `prefiero`, `tener` → `tiene`, `venir` → `viene`.
- `o` → `ue`: `poder` → `puedo`, `dormir` → `duermo`, `encontrar` → `encuentro`, `mostrar` → `muestro`, `volver` → `vuelvo`, `soñar` → `sueño`, `contar` → `cuento`.
- `e` → `i` (only some `-ir` verbs): `pedir` → `pido`, `seguir` → `sigo`, `decir` → `digo`, `elegir` → `elijo`.
- `comer` is regular — does NOT split.
- Cognate hooks: `tiempo` ↔ `tempo`, `puerto` ↔ `port`, `escuela` ↔ `school`, `cuerpo` ↔ `corp`, `concierto` ↔ `concert`.

References: 13, 15, 16, 17, 18, 40, 42, 74, 75

## Irregular present yo forms

- Only four `-oy` `yo` forms exist: `voy`, `doy`, `soy`, `estoy`.
- `-go` `yo` forms: `tengo`, `vengo`, `salgo`, `pongo`, `digo`, `hago`, `oigo`, `traigo` (with extra `i`), `valgo`, `caigo`.
- `saber` → `sé`. `ver` → `veo` (kept `e`). `conocer` → `conozco` (`-zco` family). `dar` → `doy`.
- Most `-go` `yo` forms also yield `-ga` subjunctives across all persons (`tenga`, `venga`, `diga`).

| `ir`      | `voy`     | `va`         | fully irregular                  |
| `dar`     | `doy`     | `da`         | `-er`/`-ir` endings in preterite |
| `haber`   | `he`      | `ha` / `hay` | auxiliary                        |
| `saber`   | `sé`      | `sabe`       | irregular `yo`                   |
| `ver`     | `veo`     | `ve`         | extra `e`                        |
| `conocer` | `conozco` | `conoce`     | `-zco` family                    |
| `tener`   | `tengo`   | `tiene`      | `-go` + `e` → `ie`               |

## Reflexive verbs and pronouns

- Reflexive pronouns: `me`, `te`, `se`, `nos`, `os`, `se`.
- Truly reflexive: `lavarse`, `vestirse`, `llamarse`, `dormirse`.
- Reciprocal (each other): `nos vemos`, `se hablan`.
- Pseudo-reflexive / lexical: `quedarse` (to stay), `acordarse de` (to remember), `enamorarse de`.
- Passive / inchoative `se`: `se vende` = it's sold; `se rompe` = it breaks; `se quema` = it burns.
- Impersonal `se` + 3sg verb: `¿Cómo se dice?` = how do you say it? `Se habla español`.
- Unexpected events 'on me' — `se` + `me`/`te`/`le`/`nos`/`les` + verb: `se me cierra la puerta`; `se me quemó la cena`; `se me acaba la batería`.
- Pronoun position: before a conjugated verb; attached to infinitive, gerund, or affirmative imperative.

References: 16, 25, 26, 38, 41, 47, 48, 63, 80, 81

## Object pronouns

- Direct: `me`, `te`, `lo`/`la`, `nos`, `os`, `los`/`las`.
- Indirect: `me`, `te`, `le`, `nos`, `os`, `les`.
- `me`/`te`/`nos` already cover to me, for me, from me — Spanish drops the extra preposition (`me lo compra` = he buys it for me; `me hablan` = they speak to me).
- IO precedes DO: `me lo dan` = they give it to me.
- `le` / `les` + `lo`/`la`/`los`/`las` → `se lo` / `se la` / `se los`/`se las` (avoids `le lo`).
- Position: before a conjugated verb; attached to infinitive / gerund / affirmative imperative.
- With perfect (`haber` + part.) or modals: clitic before the conjugated word OR attached to the infinitive (`lo quiero ver` = `quiero verlo`; `lo he hecho`).
- With negative imperative: clitics PRECEDE (`no me lo digas`).
- Clarify `le`/`se` with `a` + person (`se lo di a él`).

### Object pronouns

|                 | Direct      | Indirect | After preposition (stressed)    |
|-----------------|-------------|----------|---------------------------------|
| 1sg             | `me`        | `me`     | `mí` (`conmigo` with `con`)     |
| 2sg informal    | `te`        | `te`     | `ti` (`contigo` with `con`)     |
| 3sg / formal    | `lo`/`la`   | `le`     | `él`/`ella`/`usted` (`consigo`) |
| 1pl             | `nos`       | `nos`    | `nosotros`/`nosotras`           |
| 2pl Spain       | `os`        | `os`     | `vosotros`/`vosotras`           |
| 3pl / formal pl | `los`/`las` | `les`    | `ellos`/`ellas`/`ustedes`       |

References: 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 12, 21, 22, 24, 25, 34, 37, 39, 62, 72

## `gustar`-type verbs

- Construction: indirect-object pronoun + verb agreeing with the THING (subject).
- Singular thing → me gusta; plural things → me gustan.
- Other gustar verbs: encantar, interesar, parecer, doler, faltar, hacer falta, importar, quedar, sobrar.
- Clarify or stress the experiencer with a + person: A ella le gusta; A él le gustó.
- Conditional for politeness: me gustaría = I would like.
- doler + body part with article (NOT possessive): me duele la pierna.
- Past forms: gustó (event) vs gustaba (ongoing).

References: 42, 45, 61, 63, 66, 70

## Prepositions

- Common: a, de, en, con, por, para, sin, sobre, hasta, desde, entre, bajo, contra.
- After prepositions, use stressed pronouns: para mí, de ti, con él, sobre ella.
- con + mí / ti → conmigo / contigo (also consigo).
- Verb-specific prepositions: pensar EN, soñar CON, enamorarse DE, depender DE, contar CON, casarse CON, ir A.
- Spanish never strands prepositions: ¿De dónde eres? ¿A dónde vas? ¿Con quién hablas?
- para + infinitive (purpose, same subject) vs para que + subjunctive (different subject).
- gracias por + infinitive: gracias por venir.

### `por` vs `para`

|                         | por                                           | para                                |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Cause / reason          | by, because of, on behalf of (Lo hago por ti) | —                                   |
| Recipient / destination | —                                             | for, to (Es para ti)                |
| Purpose / goal          | —                                             | in order to (Estoy aquí para verte) |
| Duration of time        | for / during (por tres días)                  | deadline (para el viernes)          |
| Means / channel         | by / through (por teléfono)                   | —                                   |
| Exchange                | in exchange for (cambio este por ese)         | —                                   |
| Opinion                 | —                                             | for, in the opinion of (para mí)    |

References: 7, 8, 19, 25, 32, 39, 40, 50, 51, 52, 56, 71, 74, 81

## Personal a

- Place 'a' before a specific human (or personified) direct object: Veo a María, espero a David.
- a + el contracts to al: invito al profesor.
- Used after subjunctive triggers too: espero que vean a María.
- Not used after tener in many cases: tengo dos hermanos.

References: 56, 71

## Negation

- no goes immediately before the verb (and any clitic): no lo quiero.
- Spanish allows / requires double negatives: no comí nada, no vino nadie, no fui nunca.
- If a negative word precedes the verb, drop no: nadie vino; nunca fui.
- más + nada/nadie = anything/anybody else (no comí nada más).

References: 2, 4, 5, 59, 65

## Questions and exclamations

- Yes/no questions use intonation; ¿...? wraps the line in writing.
- Question words always carry an accent: qué, quién(es), dónde, cuándo, cómo, cuánto/a/os/as, por qué, cuál(es).
- qué = what (asks for definition); cuál = which (asks for choice).
- No stranded prepositions: ¿De dónde eres? ¿A dónde vas? ¿Con quién hablas?
- Exclamations: ¡qué + adjective + (que) + clause! — ¡Qué cansados están!

References: 8, 13, 26, 56, 67, 68

## Imperatives (commands)

- Negative commands (all persons) use the subjunctive form: no hables, no coma, no se vayan.
- Affirmative usted / ustedes: subjunctive form (hable, coma, esperen).
- Affirmative tú: drop the -s of the present tú (compras → compra; comes → come).
- Affirmative vosotros: drop -r of infinitive, add -d (hablar → hablad).
- Affirmative nosotros = let's: subjunctive (hablemos, esperemos); ¡Vamos! is also common.
- Affirmative imperatives: clitics ATTACH and may need an accent (dímelo, espérenme, hazlo).
- Negative imperatives: clitics PRECEDE the verb (no me lo digas, no se lo den).
- que + subjunctive lets a third party act: que entre, que hable.

### Irregular affirmative `tú` commands (informal)

| Verb  | Command | Verb  | Command |
|-------|---------|-------|---------|
| decir | di      | salir | sal     |
| hacer | haz     | ser   | sé      |
| ir    | ve      | tener | ten     |
| poner | pon     | venir | ven     |

## Future tense

- Three ways to talk about the future:
    1. Simple present + future-time word: Salgo mañana; Nos vemos pronto.
    2. ir + a + infinitive (going to): Voy a verlo pronto.
    3. Synthetic future: full infinitive + endings.
- Future endings (one set for -ar/-er/-ir): -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án.
- All endings carry the stress (write the accent on -é, -ás, etc.).
- Irregular future stems: tendr-, vendr-, podr-, pondr-, saldr-, sabr-, querr-, har-, dir-, habr-.
- traer is regular (traeré).

### Future tense — `comer` (regular)

|               | Form      |
|---------------|-----------|
| yo            | comeré    |
| tú            | comerás   |
| él/ella/usted | comerá    |
| nosotros      | comeremos |
| vosotros      | comeréis  |
| ellos/ustedes | comerán   |

## Conditional (would)

- Form: full infinitive (or irregular future stem) + -ía / -ías / -ía / -íamos / -íais / -ían.
- Same irregular stems as the future: podría, tendría, haría, diría, habría.
- yo and él/ella/usted share the form (bailaría).
- Polite want: me gustaría + infinitive.
- would have ... = habría + participle (Habríamos podido ir).
- should = debería + infinitive (Deberías hablar con ella).
- Counterfactual: si + past subjunctive, conditional in main (Si pudiera, lo haría).

References: 41, 42, 43, 53, 81, 83, 86

## Imperfect (line in the past)

- Used for ongoing/repeated/background past actions: was doing / used to do.
- -ar: -aba, -abas, -aba, -ábamos, -abais, -aban (cenaba).
- -er and -ir: -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían (comía, vendía, vivía).
- Only three irregulars: ser → era; ir → iba; ver → veía.
- yo and él/ella/usted share the form — add a pronoun to disambiguate.
- iba a + infinitive = was going to ...
- gustar in past: me gustaba (ongoing) vs me gustó (event).

### Imperfect — three irregulars

|---------------|--------|--------|---------|
| yo            | era    | iba    | veía    |
| tú            | eras   | ibas   | veías   |
| él/ella/usted | era    | iba    | veía    |
| nosotros      | éramos | íbamos | veíamos |
| ellos/ustedes | eran   | iban   | veían   |

References: 46, 47, 48, 49, 53, 60, 61, 87

## Preterite ('point in the past')

- Used for one-off completed events with a closed time frame.
- -ar endings: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron.
- -er and -ir endings: -í, -iste, -ió, -imos, -isteis, -ieron.
- Stress is on the ending in yo/él (note the accents on -é, -í, -ó).
- nosotros forms of -ar (-amos) and -ir (-imos) are identical to the present — context disambiguates.
- Strong (irregular) preterites take a special stem and unaccented endings -e/-iste/-o/-imos/-ieron.
- i drops in the 3pl ending after j: dijeron, trajeron, fueron.
- ver is irregular but un-stressed: vi, viste, vio, vimos, vieron (no accents).
- ir and ser share the same preterite: fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fueron.
- hace + time = X time ago: hace dos semanas.

## Compound (perfect) tenses

- All compound tenses = haber (conjugated) + past participle.
- Participle is INVARIABLE after haber (does not agree).
- -ar → -ado; -er and -ir → -ido (hablado, comido, vivido).
- Object pronouns go BEFORE haber: lo he visto.
- In Spain, present perfect is preferred for recent past where Latin America uses preterite.
- Pluperfect: había + part. = had done (Ya había comido).
- Conditional perfect: habría + part. = would have done.
- Past subjunctive perfect: hubiera + part. (si me lo hubieras contado).
- debería / podría haber + part. = should/could have done. Clitic floats: lo debería haber hecho = debería haberlo hecho.

## Progressive (estar + gerund)

- Use ONLY for what's happening right now; not for a planned future.
- Form: -ar → -ando; -er/-ir → -iendo (hablando, comiendo, viviendo).
- Stem changes in some -ir gerunds: dormir → durmiendo; pedir → pidiendo; decir → diciendo.
- y replaces i between vowels: leer → leyendo; oír → oyendo.
- Even trait verbs allow a 'right now' progressive: estoy siendo sincero.
- Object pronouns: lo estoy preparando OR estoy preparándolo (accent kept).

References: 34, 35, 44, 52, 78

## Subjunctive ('mood tense')

- Form from the yo present indicative: drop -o, swap to opposite-vowel endings (-ar verbs use -e, -er/-ir use -a).
- Triggers: subordinate clauses after volition, emotion, doubt, unrealized situations + que.
- quiero que + subj.; necesito que; espero que; prefiero que; me gusta que; hace falta que.
- Impersonal expressions: es + adjective + que (es posible / importante / increíble) → subjunctive.
- BUT lo + adjective + es que → indicative (lo increíble es que vuelven).
- ojalá + subj. = hopefully (Ojalá deje un mensaje).
- Time clauses: cuando + indicative for habitual; cuando + subj. for FUTURE event (cuando vayamos).
- Purpose: para + inf. (same subject) vs para que + subj. (different subject).
- Indefinite / 'whatever, wherever, whoever': donde quieras, como quieras, lo que prefieras.
- Indefinite existence: ¿Hay alguien aquí que hable español? — subjunctive when uncertain.
- Imperatives use subjunctive forms (except positive informal tú/vosotros).
- Past subjunctive: take ellos preterite, drop -ron, add -ra/-ras/-ra/-ramos/-ran (also -se forms exist).
- After past triggers + que → past subjunctive (quería que comieran).
- Counterfactual si + past subj., conditional in main (si pudiera, lo haría).
- Pluperfect subjunctive: hubiera + part. (si me lo hubieras contado, habría ido).

## Conjunctions and connectors

- y = and (becomes e before a word starting with i/hi).
- o = or (becomes u before a word starting with o/ho).
- pero = but; sino = but rather (after a negation).
- porque = because; por qué = why (two words, accented).
- si = if; sí = yes (accent distinguishes).
- que = that — often required where English drops it.
- cuando = when (relative); cuándo = when (question).
- Time / sequence: ya, todavía, aún, luego, después, entonces, mientras, mientras tanto.
- Adversative / contrast: aunque, sin embargo, no obstante.
- También = also; tampoco = neither.

References: 9, 19, 30, 50, 67, 73

## Relative pronouns

- que = that / which / who (most common; for things and people).
- quien / quienes = who (after preposition or set off by commas).
- el cual / la cual / los cuales / las cuales — formal alternative after preposition.
- lo que = what / that which (no quiero hablar de lo que pasó).
- cuyo / cuya / cuyos / cuyas = whose (agrees with thing owned).
- donde = where (relative); cuándo = when (question only).

References: 56, 70, 75, 82

## Numbers, time, quantifiers

- Common time words: ahora, hoy, mañana, ayer, anoche, esta noche, más tarde, pronto, ya, todavía.
- la semana que viene = next week; el mes pasado = last month; el año que viene = next year.
- una vez = once; otra vez = again; a la vez = at the same time; cuántas veces = how many times.
- siempre, nunca, casi nunca, a veces, a menudo, normalmente.
- durante = during; hace + time = ago; desde hace + time = for (still ongoing).
- Quantity: mucho, poco, bastante, demasiado, todo, nada.
- demasiado has special agreement: agrees with following noun, invariable before another adjective.
- Numbers 1–10: uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez (Roman calendar mnemonics: ocho → octagon/October, nueve → November, diez → December).

References: 6, 18, 19, 20, 21, 25, 30, 50, 51, 53, 58, 59, 60, 64, 65, 89

## False friends and useful idioms

- discutir = to argue (not 'discuss' in English sense; use hablar / debatir for that).
- actual = current; actualmente = currently (not 'actually'; use de hecho / en realidad).
- realizar = to carry out; 'to realize (mentally)' = darse cuenta.
- asistir a = to attend (not 'assist'; for 'help' use ayudar).
- embarazada = pregnant (not 'embarrassed').
- éxito = success; salir = to leave / go out (not 'exit').
- sensible = sensitive; sensato = sensible/reasonable.
- nada más = nothing else; nadie más = nobody else.

References: 37, 51

## Dialect notes

- vosotros (informal pl) is Spain only; Latin America uses ustedes for both formal and informal pl.
- leísmo (Spain): le veo for 'I see him' (some regions also for 'her').
- voseo (Argentina, Uruguay, parts of Central America): vos replaces tú with end-stressed forms (vos podés, vos querés).
- c/z = /θ/ in most of Spain, /s/ in Latin America (gracias, cinco, razón).
- Coastal/Caribbean accents aspirate or drop final s ([ehtá] for está).
- Words for 'car': coche (Spain), carro (most Latin America), auto (Southern Cone), máquina (some places).
- Many adjectives go either way with ser/estar depending on dialect (casado, ocupado).
